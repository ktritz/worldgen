# Subterranean / Enclosed Civilization Energy & Food Mechanisms

Survey of mechanisms NOT already in our six (radiotrophic minerals, chemolithotrophic strata,
geothermal, ley-field, deep-root parasitism, wrought/divine light). Sourcing note: session web-search
budget was exhausted; rows marked (unv.) are from model knowledge and were not re-verified against
a primary source. Derinkuyu and Dwarf Fortress rows were fetched and verified.

| # | Mechanism | Source work | Energy/food basis | Depletes? | Strategic implication (relation to surface) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Allochthonous detritus / guano economy** | Real cave ecology (bat guano, flood-washed litter); marine snow | Surface primary production falls in as waste; full food web on it | Yes — collapses if surface biome dies | **One-way, non-reciprocal dependence.** No trade, no contact, surface unaware it feeds them. Deforestation topside = famine below with no diplomatic channel. The most important omission. |
| B | **Fungiculture as *converter*, not source** | Leafcutter ants (real); myconids, DF plump helmets, Metro 2033 mushroom farms (unv.) | Fungus converts imported biomass/necromass; produces no energy itself | Yes — tracks substrate inflow | Forces a **bulk low-value import** (biomass) paid for with high-value exports. Creates substrate chokepoints, tariffs, a carrier caste. Flag: most "fungus civilizations" are secretly running on this. |
| C | **Mineral rent — buy food, don't grow it** | Tolkien (Erebor/Dale, Moria); Warhammer dwarfs; Menzoberranzan via Skullport (unv.) | Metal/gems/craft exported for grain | No (until the seam runs out) | **Mutual dependence + embargo as a weapon.** Merchant intermediary settlements. The dominant fantasy answer and absent from our six. |
| D | **Predatory husbandry** | *The Time Machine* (Morlocks farm the Eloi) | Surface population is the livestock, deliberately kept fertile | Self-limiting | Underground **invests in surface prosperity** and suppresses surface development. Opposite of raiding: peace maintained as a herding policy. |
| E | **Raid/tribute extraction** | Skaven; drow slave raids (unv.) | Periodic seizure of surface calories and bodies | No, but provokes retaliation | Cyclic war economy; surface builds walls, not markets. Distinct political mode from C. |
| F | **Grazer-vector livestock** | Wells, *First Men in the Moon* (mooncalves); real bats/swiftlets | Herd forages topside, is milked/slaughtered below | Tracks surface pasture | Extracts surface biomass **without the polity appearing**. Surface pest-control is an unwitting act of war. |
| G | **Lithovore livestock exporting fuel** | Oxygen Not Included (hatches eat rock, excrete coal) (unv.) | Animals convert mineral to combustible commodity | Slowly (strata) | **Inverts dependency** — the surface buys energy from below. Underground becomes a fuel exporter, not a supplicant. |
| H | **Stockpile / closed-loop vault on a clock** | *Silo/Wool*, Fallout vaults, *The City of Ember* (unv.) | Engineered capital: hydroponics, recycling, stored stock | Yes, catastrophically, on a known clock | **Deliberate isolation; surface is myth or taboo.** No trade by ideology. Ends in a forced emergence event — a scripted political rupture. |
| I | **Failing legacy infrastructure** | Forster, *The Machine Stops*; Ember's generator | An artifact provides everything; repair knowledge is lost | Yes, systemically | Like H but failure is **maintenance-driven, not stock-driven**: an engineer caste holds real power; competence, not rationing, is the political axis. |
| J | **Subterranean hydropower / mechanical flow** | *The City of Ember* (river generator) (unv.) | River, tide, or chimney-effect airflow drives machinery | No, but location-locked | Settlement is **pinned to one geological feature**; capturing the flow captures the polity. Non-depleting energy without solving food. |
| K | **Mined fossil fuel** | Metro 2033 diesel; *Mortal Engines* fuel hunger (unv.) | Burn hydrocarbons/uranium found below to run lights and heat | Yes | Motivates **outward expansion for fuel**, not food. Underground becomes expansionist and mobile. |
| L | **Refuge cycling / siege larder** | Derinkuyu (verified: presses, cellars, stables, 55 m well, 20k capacity, occupied only in danger) | Surface harvest stored below; wells; intermittent occupation | Yes, on a siege clock | Underground is **a function of surface warfare**, not a separate people. Population oscillates between levels. |
| M | **Below-ground housing, above-ground fields** | Coober Pedy, Matmata, Cappadocia, yaodong (unv.) | Ordinary surface agriculture; the underground is thermal/defensive shelter | No | **There is no separate underground polity.** The single most realistic and most-missed case: subterranean settlement as climate adaptation. |
| N | **Piped/reflected daylight (lightwells, mirrors)** | Matmata pit-courtyards; sun-shaft architecture (unv.) | Real sunlight relayed underground to grow real crops | No, but weather-coupled | Underground is **surface-dependent for light but self-sufficient for food**; shaft-mouths are strategic terrain to be seized or blocked. |
| O | **Waste-stream / discard economy** | Miéville, *Un Lun Dun* ("moil"); Metro bullet-currency (unv.) | Lives on what the surface throws away or abandons | Tracks surface affluence | Prospers when the surface is rich and wasteful; **wants the surface strong** but has no leverage over it. |
| P | **Warpstone-type toxic supercharger** | Warhammer Skaven (unv.) | Mutagenic mineral supplements calories/power | Yes, and it is the object of conquest | Boom-bust population, addiction, and a **casus belli that is the fuel itself**. |
| Q | **Non-eating population** | Persephone myth (underworld food binds you); undead/construct polities; Hades' asphodel (unv.) | The consumer is removed: spirits, undead, golems need no calories | N/A | **Immune to embargo, siege, and famine.** Trades labor, not grain. Persephone variant: eating below makes emigration impossible, so population grows only by capture. |
| R | **External metropole resupply** | Deep Rock Galactic (orbital resupply pods) (unv.) | Nothing is grown; an outside power ships everything in | Ends when the sponsor leaves | Underground is a **company outpost, never sovereign**. Its politics are the sponsor's politics. |
| S | **Hive/ranching symbiosis** | RimWorld insect hives and jelly; DF cavern fauna (unv.) | A kept hive produces food from ambiguous input | Unclear by design | Cheap calories with an internal predation risk — the food supply can turn on the polity. |

## Variants of our six, not distinct

- **Hydrothermal vent chemosynthesis** = our #2, but with a distinct *geography*: point-source oases, sharp gradients, vents dying in decades → forces nomadism and dispersed, short-lived settlements. Add as a modifier on #2, not a new row.
- **Faerzress** (Underdark ambient magic) = our #4. Note it does *not* feed anyone in canon; it warps magic and hides scrying. Cite as precedent for #4 only. (unv.)
- **Nidhogg gnawing Yggdrasil's root** = our #5. Best mythic precedent to cite.
- **Journey to the Centre of the Earth** ambient electric "sky" = our #6/#4; the Lidenbrock Sea is a relict biosphere, not a new energy basis.
- **RimWorld/Subnautica sun-lamp hydroponics** = our #6 with mechanism K supplying the power.
- **Deep rothé / DF cavern trees** = unexplained autotrophy; settings hand-wave the base. In a simulator you must pick A, B, or N underneath them.
- **Deliberate scarcity** (Silo lottery, Ember rationing) is a *policy layer*, not an energy source — model separately as a carrying-capacity governor.

## Verdict — genuinely distinct additions

Add these nine, ranked by how much new political behaviour they buy:

1. **C — mineral rent / buy food with metal.** Biggest gap. Produces trade routes, embargoes, and intermediary cities.
2. **A — allochthonous detritus.** Dependence with no diplomacy; surface holds unwitting leverage.
3. **M — below-ground housing, surface fields.** Removes the "separate underground polity" assumption entirely; the realistic default.
4. **D — predatory husbandry.** Underground that *protects and cultivates* the surface. No other mechanism gives this.
5. **H/I — vault stockpile vs. failing infrastructure.** Isolation by ideology, ending in a forced emergence event; I differs from H by making an engineer caste sovereign.
6. **Q — non-eating population.** Immune to siege and embargo; breaks every economic lever the surface has.
7. **G — lithovore livestock / energy export.** Inverts the dependency so the surface needs the underground.
8. **R — external metropole resupply.** A non-sovereign underground; useful for colonial/extractive scenarios.
9. **B — fungus as converter.** Not an energy source; adopt as a *rule* that every fungal economy must name its substrate.

Secondary but cheap to add: **E** (raid), **L** (siege larder), **N** (lightwells), **K** (fossil fuel), **O** (waste-stream), **P** (toxic supercharger), **J** (flow power), **F** (grazer-vector), **S** (hive).
